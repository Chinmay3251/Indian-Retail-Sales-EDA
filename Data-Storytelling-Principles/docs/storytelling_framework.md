# Data Storytelling Framework

## Audience and decision
Identify who will use the analysis, what they already know, and what decision they need to make.

## Central question
Focus on one decision-relevant question, rather than listing every possible observation.
Example: Which product categories and regions should the sales team review to improve profitable growth?

## Story arc
1. **Context:** What is happening, and why does it matter?
2. **Problem or opportunity:** What needs attention?
3. **Evidence:** Which metrics, comparisons, and charts support the observation?
4. **Insight:** What does the evidence mean — the “so what”?
5. **Action:** What should the audience do next?
6. **Measurement:** Which metric will show whether the action worked?

## Finding-to-action structure
- Observation: state the measurable pattern.
- Evidence: give the value, comparison, time period, and chart.
- Interpretation: explain why it matters.
- Action: recommend a realistic next step.

## Visual selection
- Line chart: trends over time.
- Bar chart: category or region comparisons.
- Scatter plot: relationships and outliers.
- KPI card: one important number.
- Table: exact values when detail is needed.

Use takeaway-led titles, clear labels, units, consistent scales, and minimal decoration.

## Good practice
Lead with the main takeaway. Separate facts, interpretation, and recommendations. Avoid implying causation from correlation. Include period, metric definitions, and limitations. Do not hide contradictory evidence or uncertainty.
